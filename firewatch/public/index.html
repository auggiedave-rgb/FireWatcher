    About the data

    Detections come from NASA FIRMS (Fire Information for Resource Management System). Thermal infrared sensors on polar-orbiting satellites flag pixels that are much hotter than their surroundings.

    • VIIRS (NOAA-20, NOAA-21, Suomi NPP): 375 m pixels, the most sensitive to small fires.
    • MODIS (Terra, Aqua): 1 km pixels, longest historical record.
    • Each satellite passes over a given place roughly twice a day (day and night). Data usually arrives within 3 hours of the pass.

    How to read a point

    • A point marks the centre of a hot pixel, not the exact fire position or its size.
    • FRP (Fire Radiative Power, MW) measures how much heat the fire radiates: a proxy for its intensity.
    • Confidence reflects how likely the detection is a real fire.

    Limits

    • These are thermal anomalies: volcanoes, gas flares, industrial plants and hot surfaces can also appear.
    • Thick clouds and dense smoke can hide fires; no detection does not mean no fire.
    • For emergencies always rely on official channels (in Italy: Protezione Civile, Vigili del Fuoco – 115; in the EU: EFFIS / Copernicus EMS).
